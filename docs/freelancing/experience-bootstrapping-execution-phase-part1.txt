(`RAG-Powered Customer Support Chatbot`) as if it were a real client engagement,

6. Execution of milestones with "client" check-ins

as if the client were real. Treat this as a rehearsal you can copy-paste from

1.1: SIMULATED CLIENT PROFILE FOR PROJECT 1
-------------------------------------------

To keep the simulation realistic and reusable, you will define a concrete
fictional client and stick to it throughout the project. Treat this profile as
if it had come from a real Upwork job post and a short initial message.

CLIENT SNAPSHOT
• Company: AcmeDesk
• Industry: B2B SaaS (customer support platform)
• Size: ~50 employees (Series A/B startup)
• Existing Product: Web-based helpdesk with ticketing and a public knowledge base
• Tech Stack (assumed): React front-end, Node.js/Express API, PostgreSQL database

PAIN POINTS (BASED ON PHASE 1 OBSERVATION)
• Support team handles ~200 tickets per day, many are repetitive FAQs
• Average first response time is 2–3 hours during business hours, slower at night
• Knowledge base exists but is underutilized; customers prefer to open tickets
• Management wants 24/7 support without hiring a full second-shift team

PROJECT GOAL (CLIENT LANGUAGE)
• "We want an AI chatbot on our website that can answer customer questions using
  our existing FAQs, product docs, and support articles so that our team can
  focus on complex tickets."

SIMULATED INITIAL JOB POST (UPWORK-STYLE)
----------------------------------------
TITLE:
• "RAG-Powered Customer Support Chatbot for SaaS Help Center"

DESCRIPTION:
• "We are a B2B SaaS company with ~3,000 active customers. Our support team
  handles ~200 tickets/day, many of which are repetitive questions already
  answered in our documentation (FAQs, product guides, troubleshooting docs).
  We want an AI chatbot on our website that:
  • Answers customer questions using OUR knowledge base (not generic ChatGPT)
  • Reduces repetitive tickets so agents can focus on complex issues
  • Provides safe, non-hallucinated answers with source links
  • Can be managed by our non-technical support team (upload docs, view stats)

  We already have:
  • Public help center with ~120 articles (mixed HTML + PDF)
  • Internal product documentation in Google Docs

  Deliverables for v1:
  • Embedded chat widget on our marketing site
  • RAG backend connected to our docs
  • Simple admin panel for document uploads and basic analytics

  Please share:
  • Links to similar chatbots you've built (especially RAG-powered)
  • Rough timeline and budget estimate for a v1."

1.2: STANDARD ENGAGEMENT FLOW (END-TO-END)
------------------------------------------

This subsection defines the generic flow you will follow for this and future
projects. For Project 1 you will "play through" each step explicitly.

HIGH-LEVEL ENGAGEMENT STAGES
1. Inbound & Qualification
   • Client posts a job or messages you directly.
   • You quickly assess: niche fit, budget sanity, timeline reasonableness.
   • Outcome: Decide whether to proceed to a discovery call.

  EXAMPLE: QUICK QUALIFICATION CHECKLIST FOR PROJECT 1
  • Niche fit:
    - RAG-powered chatbot? YES (matches primary + entry niches).
  • Budget sanity:
    - They say "v1" for a SaaS help center; typical range from observation
      phase: $3,000–$8,000+.
    - If they expect <$1,000, you would push back or decline.
  • Timeline reasonableness:
    - 3 weeks for an MVP with clear scope is realistic.
  • Data situation:
    - They already have an existing help center and docs: good sign.

  If at least 3 of 4 look good, you proceed to propose a call.

  SAMPLE REPLY TO INITIAL MESSAGE
  ------------------------------
  "Hi Sarah,

  Thanks for reaching out and for the detailed context—your use case is a
  perfect fit for what I do. A RAG-powered chatbot over your help center and
  internal docs is exactly the type of system I specialize in.

  I suggest we start with a short 20–30 minute call to clarify:
  • Your success metrics (e.g. target reduction in ticket volume / response time)
  • The current state of your documentation (formats, where it lives today)
  • Constraints around brand voice, hallucinations and data privacy
  • What feels realistic for a first 3-week v1

  This will let me come back with a concise plan that includes:
  • A plain-language architecture (how the chatbot, RAG backend and your existing
    systems would connect)
  • Milestones with concrete deliverables you can review on a staging URL
  • Timeline, budget and what I need from your team

  I’m available on:
  • Tuesday between 15:00–18:00 CET
  • Wednesday between 10:00–13:00 CET

  Please let me know which slot works best for you, or suggest another time.

  Best,
  Ted"

2. Discovery / Clarification Call
   • 20–30 minute video call.
   • Objectives:
     - Clarify business goals, constraints, and success metrics.
     - Understand data sources, current systems, and internal ownership.
     - Narrow down scope suitable for a first milestone / v1.
   • Output: Written call recap within 24 hours.

  SAMPLE DISCOVERY CALL AGENDA (WHAT YOU SEND BEFORE THE CALL)
  -----------------------------------------------------------
  Subject: "AcmeDesk AI Chatbot – Discovery Call Agenda"

  "Hi Sarah,

  Looking forward to our call. To make sure we cover the essentials and respect
  your time, here is a short agenda:

  1) Current support workflow (5–7 min)
     • How tickets come in today (channels, volume)
     • Where your docs live (help center, internal docs)

  2) Goals and constraints for the chatbot (7–10 min)
     • What would make this project a clear success in 3–6 months?
     • Any strict constraints around tone, compliance, or data residency?

  3) Data and technical environment (5–7 min)
     • Formats and systems I should integrate with in v1 (and what can wait)
     • Who on your side would own content and approvals?

  4) Scope of a 3-week v1 (5–7 min)
     • Prioritizing a small, high-impact slice we can ship quickly
     • How we’d organize milestones and reviews

  At the end I’ll recap what I heard and outline next steps: a short written
  plan with architecture, milestones, and a proposal.

  Best,
  Ted"

  SAMPLE CALL NOTES STRUCTURE (WHAT YOU KEEP FOR YOURSELF)
  --------------------------------------------------------
  • Participants: Sarah (Head of Support), you
  • Date/time:
  • Recording link (if any):

  1) Current situation
     - Channels: Intercom widget + email

  2) Goals

     - Avoid "AI gone wrong" moments (no invented policies)

  3) Constraints

  4) v1 scope ideas (draft)
     - Chat widget for FAQs + product questions
     - RAG over public help center + selected internal docs
     - Basic admin panel: upload docs, see top queries

  5) Next steps you promise
     - Send recap + architecture sketch + milestones within 24 hours
     - Include budget/timeline options

3. Written Proposal & Architecture Summary
   • You send a short, client-friendly proposal that includes:
     - Problem summary in the client's language.
     - Plain-language architecture for the chatbot + RAG backend.
     - Milestones (3–5), each with deliverables and acceptance criteria.
     - Timeline and budget (fixed price or estimate + hourly).
     - Assumptions, dependencies, and what you need from the client.

  STRUCTURE OF YOUR 1–2 PAGE PROPOSAL EMAIL
  -----------------------------------------
  Subject: "Proposal – RAG-Powered Support Chatbot for AcmeDesk (3-Week v1)"

  1) Problem & goal (client language)
     • 2–3 sentences restating what you heard:
       "Your support team is handling ~200 tickets/day, with ~60% being
        repetitive questions already answered in your docs. Our goal for this
        first 3-week phase is to ship a chatbot that can safely answer those
        questions using your help center + selected internal docs, reducing
        repetitive tickets while maintaining CSAT."

  2) Plain-language architecture
     • Short paragraph + bullet list describing:
       - Chat widget embedded on your site
       - RAG backend that indexes your docs in a vector database
       - Guardrails to avoid hallucinations and escalate unclear cases

  3) Milestones
     • Milestone 1: Foundation & basic chatbot (week 1)
     • Milestone 2: RAG + knowledge base integration (week 2)
     • Milestone 3: Admin panel, analytics, polish & launch (week 3)
     • For each: deliverables, demo link, acceptance criteria.

  4) Timeline & budget
     • Example: fixed price of $8,000 split across milestones:
       - M1: $2,500
       - M2: $3,000
       - M3: $2,500

  5) Assumptions & client responsibilities
     • Docs will be provided in agreed formats by a certain date.
     • A single point of contact for decisions.
     • You’ll have sandbox access if any existing systems need to be touched.

  6) Next step
     • "If this outline looks aligned, I can set up the contract with these
        three milestones. Once the first milestone is funded, I’ll share the
        project board and start immediately."

4. Scope & Commercial Agreement
   • Client approves proposal or requests adjustments.
   • You lock in:
     - Final scope for Milestone 1 (and outline for later milestones).
     - Commercial terms (fixed vs hourly; payment schedule; currency).
     - Communication expectations (response times, meeting cadence).
   • On a platform like Upwork: this is a formal contract with funded
     milestone(s).

  EXAMPLE: HOW YOU EXPLAIN BILLING TO THE CLIENT
  ---------------------------------------------
  "For this project I recommend a fixed price of $8,000 broken into three
   milestones that map directly to the architecture we discussed:

   • Milestone 1 – Foundation & Base Chat (Week 1) – $2,500
     - Outcome: Embedded chat UI talking to a basic LLM backend (no RAG yet).
     - You can click around and see how the chatbot will feel in production.

   • Milestone 2 – RAG Knowledge Base Integration (Week 2) – $3,000
     - Outcome: Chatbot grounded in your help center + selected internal docs.
     - We’ll review retrieval quality and tune chunking / search together.

   • Milestone 3 – Admin Panel, Analytics & Launch (Week 3) – $2,500
     - Outcome: Admin dashboard, basic analytics, production deployment and
       handover session.

   On Upwork, each milestone would be funded before I start that phase and
   released once you’ve reviewed the staging environment and we’ve resolved
   any critical issues for that slice. This keeps risk low on both sides."

5. Project Setup
   • Create:
     - Private GitHub repository.
     - Project board (tasks, statuses, milestones).
     - Shared documentation space (Notion / Google Docs).
   • Configure:
     - Environments (dev, staging; production for launch).
     - Secrets management (.env, managed secrets).

6. Execution by Milestones
   • For each milestone:
     - Break work into tasks.
     - Implement incrementally.
     - Provide short, regular updates (written + occasional calls).
     - Deliver to a staging URL for review.
     - Collect feedback and make agreed adjustments.

7. Launch & Handover
   • Deploy to production (or client's preferred environment).
   • Provide:
     - Technical documentation (README, architecture notes, runbooks).
     - Admin usage docs (how to upload docs, manage settings).
     - Short live walkthrough / handover call.
   • Agree on:
     - Post-launch support window (e.g., 2 weeks of bug fixes).
     - Options for ongoing maintenance or future feature phases.

During this simulation you will explicitly draft:
• A sample initial reply to the inbound message.
• A discovery call agenda and call notes.
• A one-page proposal + architecture summary for Project 1.
• A milestone + billing breakdown aligned with your target rates.

1.3: COMMUNICATION PRINCIPLES AND CADENCE
-----------------------------------------

Professional AI projects fail more often from poor communication than poor code.
This subsection defines how you will communicate during the Project 1 simulation
and later with real clients.

CORE PRINCIPLES
• Clarity over jargon:
  - Explain RAG, embeddings, and vector DBs in plain business language first.
  - Only dive into technical depth when the client asks or is clearly technical.
• Frequent, small updates:
  - Short written updates beat long silence.
  - Default: 2–3 updates per week during active milestones.
• Expectation management:
  - Be explicit about what is in scope, what is not, and what "v1" means.
  - Surface risks and unknowns early (e.g., data quality, API limits).
• Single source of truth:
  - Keep one central place with current scope, milestones, and links
    (e.g., a Notion "Project Home" or a Google Doc).

SIMULATED CADENCE FOR PROJECT 1
• Before contract:
  - 1 discovery call.
  - 1 follow-up email with recap + proposal.
• Week 1 (Foundation and Setup):
  - Kickoff message summarizing agreed plan and this week's focus.
  - Mid-week update (what's done, what's next, blockers).
  - End-of-week update with a short Loom-style script you could record later.
• Week 2 (RAG Implementation):
  - At least 2 written updates (early in week, just before RAG demo).
  - Optional 15–20 min check-in call when first end-to-end RAG is ready.
• Week 3 (Polish and Deployment):
  - Early-week update focusing on admin panel + analytics progress.
  - Pre-launch confirmation message (what will go live, when).
  - Post-launch message with links, credentials, and next-steps.

For this simulation you will actually write:
• Template messages for:
  - Discovery call invite.
  - Post-call recap + next steps.
  - Weekly progress updates.
  - Launch / handover summary.
